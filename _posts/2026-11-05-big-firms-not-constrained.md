Why big firms are less financially constrained? A significantly overlooked dimension is on its market power. Given that all firms are connected through the supply chain network, both big and small firms have suppliers and customers. Different from the small players in the economy, large firms generally have greater power to financing from their supply chain network. 

- they can finance from their suppliers through invoicing, 
- and finance from their customers through prepayments. 

For the big firms financing from its supply chain network, the first usually increases accounts payables and the second increases deferred revenue. By delaying cash flow outflows and accelerating cash flow inflows, these firms can keep minimum cash for its operations. This approach is closely associated with the Japanese management model (e.g. Toyota), particularly the principles of *just-in-time (JIT) production* and lean inventory management, which have influenced global business practices for several decades. By minimizing inventory holdings, improving supply-chain coordination, and optimizing payment terms, firms can significantly reduce their working-capital requirements.

However, the financial benefits of these practices are not distributed equally across the supply chain. Large firms often possess greater bargaining power, enabling them to negotiate extended payment terms with suppliers while securing favorable payment arrangements with customers. Consequently, they can shift part of their working-capital financing burden onto smaller firms within their supply-chain networks.
